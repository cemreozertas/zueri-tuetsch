For German speakers in and around Zurich

You already speak most of it. You just can't hear it yet.

Zurich German (Züritüütsch) is not a variant of Standard German (Hochdeutsch) but a close relative: both descend from medieval German and developed along their own paths. Seen through a translator's eye, most of the differences follow patterns. This site is a practical guide to hearing and speaking it, for anyone who already speaks German and wants to feel at home in the language of the city.

Why this exists

This guide was made by a multilingual translator turned functional consultant, with Turkish roots, a career in German and English, and a lasting fascination with how two languages can grow from the same roots and still sound worlds apart. After a decade of studying and working in Germany, a new chapter took her to Zurich, where she started picking up words from day one. With a translator's habit of looking for patterns and a musician's ear for sound, she noticed how regularly Zurich German lines up with Standard German, and kept listening more closely. Everything that helped her along the way is collected here: the sound patterns, the everyday words, practice, a learning plan and the story behind the dialect. It's for anyone who wants to hear and speak Zurich German faster.

The sound shifts

Each rule pairs a Hochdeutsch sound with the Züritüütsch sound you hear in its place, with examples and a tip for hearing it in reverse. The number stays the same everywhere in the app.

Download the cheat sheet (PDF)

The rules are a learner's shortcut, not history: Zurich German didn't come from Hochdeutsch. Both grew out of medieval German, and for the vowels it was actually Hochdeutsch that changed, while Zurich German kept the older sound. The Story tab explains how.
